Three states. You only notice two.

shima sits exactly over the notch and draws nothing extra until there’s something to show. When there isn’t, it gets out of the way again.

  1. Idle

    Just your notch

One island. A few small jobs.

shima does a handful of things, quietly, and never takes focus from the app you’re in.

The wings

Album art and an equalizer, in the strip of menu bar the notch already takes. They only exist while music is playing, so a quiet Mac looks exactly like it did before.

The open island

Pure black with concave corners that melt into the menu bar, and a spring that feels like the iPhone one. Home, Music, Camera and Calendar sit along the top edge; the Music tab’s progress bar ticks once a second, and only while the island is open.

Real controls

Previous, play/pause and next go straight to Spotify. Clicking them doesn’t pull you out of your editor or your browser.

A camera mirror

Open the Camera tab for a live, mirrored preview, to check yourself and your background before a call. The camera runs only while that tab is open, so the green light goes out the moment the island closes.

Calendar and reminders

Today’s date, what’s on and what’s due, with a month grid beside it, read from the Calendar and Reminders apps you already use. Anything synced there shows up, and you can tick a reminder off from the island.

Device banners

When AirPods, a keyboard or a mouse connects, a small banner drops under the notch with its name and battery, then folds away after three seconds. Hovering it opens the island, and a switch in Settings turns it off.

A Settings window

Launch at login, Quit, Spotify’s status with a shortcut to the right privacy pane, and the equalizer mode: animated, only when open, or off. Open it by launching Shima again, or from the Home icon on the island.

Small and hackable

Native Swift and SwiftUI, built with SwiftPM, with no third-party dependencies. The logic lives in a tested core library, so it’s easy to bend into your own thing.

Privacy and cost

shima never launches Spotify, only goes online to fetch album artwork, and does nothing at all while it’s idle.

No timers, no polling, no analytics, no account. Free. Your camera, calendar and reminders are read on your Mac and never sent anywhere.

  • It listens, it doesn’t poll

    Track changes arrive as Spotify’s own notifications, and calendar changes and Bluetooth connections arrive the same way. If there’s nothing to show, shima stays invisible.

  • Permissions, asked when needed

    Spotify control the first time you play something, the camera the first time you open the Camera tab, Calendar and Reminders when you open that tab, and Bluetooth so it can see devices connect. Say no to any and shima tells you where to change your mind.

  • Artwork and optional updates

    Shima fetches the cover image Spotify points to over HTTPS. When you click Check for updates, it also contacts GitHub for release information and update downloads. There is no analytics or tracking.

Questions.

What is shima?

A small Mac app that turns the MacBook notch into a Dynamic Island, like the one on the iPhone. Today it shows what Spotify is playing, a camera mirror, your calendar and reminders, and a banner when AirPods or a keyboard connects. It’s a personal project: small, local and easy to hack on.

Is it free?

Yes. No price, no account, no trial. The source is on GitHub.

Why does macOS warn me when I open it?

shima is ad-hoc signed and not notarized by Apple, so Gatekeeper blocks the first double-click. Right-click it and choose Open once, or use the one-line Terminal command in the first-launch steps.

Does it work with Apple Music or other players?

Not yet. Version 0.1.10 talks to the Spotify desktop app only for music. Without Spotify running, the wings simply don’t appear, and the camera and calendar are separate tabs.

Will it slow my Mac down?

It’s built not to. While idle, it runs no timers and does no polling. While music plays it’s event-driven, and the equalizer runs at a low frame rate; you can turn it off in Settings. The camera runs only while the Camera tab is open.

What about external monitors or a Mac without a notch?

shima only uses the built-in display for now. On a built-in screen without a notch it becomes an invisible hover zone at the top centre. External monitors and clamshell mode are still on the list.

Where is it once it’s running?

There’s no Dock icon. Launch Shima again from Spotlight or Finder to open Settings, or click the Home icon on the open island. Quit from Settings, or from the small capsule icon in the menu bar if your menu bar has room for it.

What does it ask permission for?

Spotify control (Automation) the first time you play something, and the camera the first time you open the Camera tab. It also asks for Calendar and Reminders when you open that tab, and Bluetooth so it can see a device connect. Each is asked once, only when the feature is used, and shima points you to the right System Settings pane if you say no.

What does “shima” mean?

島, shima, is Japanese for “island”. A small island in the notch.

Opening shima the first time.

Three steps, once. After that it opens like any other app.

Install

  1. Move it to Applications. Download the release, open the DMG and drag Shima into Applications (or unzip the ZIP and move Shima.app there). Launch at login and the Spotify permission want it to live in Applications.
  2. Right-click, then Open. In Applications, right-click Shima, choose Open, then Open again. If macOS only offers Done, go to System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway.
  3. Play something. Start Spotify and play a song. macOS asks once whether Shima may control Spotify: click Allow, and the wings appear. Hover the notch and click the camera icon to try the Camera tab.
Or skip step 2 in Terminal, after step 1
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Shima.app

Good to know

  • No Dock icon. shima is an accessory app. Launch it again from Spotlight to open Settings.
  • Said no to Spotify? Settings shows the status and has a button to the Automation pane in System Settings.
  • Updating. Each build is ad-hoc signed, so after installing a new version macOS may ask for the Spotify permission again.
  • Quitting. Use Quit in Settings, or run pkill -x Shima.
