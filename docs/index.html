Small on purpose. Spotify, for now.

Version 0.1.1 does one thing: the song that’s playing. It does it quietly, and it never takes focus from the app you’re in.

The wings

Album art and an equalizer, in the strip of menu bar the notch already takes. They only exist while music is playing, so a quiet Mac looks exactly like it did before.

The open island

Pure black with concave corners that melt into the menu bar, and a spring that feels like the iPhone one. The progress bar ticks once a second, and only while the island is open.

Real controls

Previous, play/pause and next go straight to Spotify. Clicking them doesn’t pull you out of your editor or your browser.

A Settings window

Launch at login, Quit, Spotify’s status with a shortcut to the right privacy pane, and the equalizer mode: animated, only when open, or off. Open it by launching Shima again, or from the gear on the island.

Built-in display

The island lives on your MacBook’s own screen. On a built-in display without a notch it becomes an invisible hover zone at the top centre. External monitors aren’t handled yet.

Small and hackable

Native Swift and SwiftUI, built with SwiftPM, with no third-party dependencies. The logic lives in a tested core library, so it’s easy to bend into your own thing.

Privacy and cost

shima never launches Spotify, only goes online to fetch album artwork, and does nothing at all while it’s idle.

No timers, no polling, no analytics, no account. Free, and it all stays on your Mac.

  • It listens, it doesn’t poll

    Track changes arrive as Spotify’s own notifications. If Spotify isn’t running, shima leaves it alone and stays invisible.

  • One permission

    macOS asks once whether shima may control Spotify (Automation). Say no and the island tells you where to change your mind.

  • Artwork, and that’s it

    The only network request is the cover image Spotify points to, over HTTPS. Nothing about you or your music leaves the Mac.

Questions.

What is shima?

A small Mac app that turns the MacBook notch into a Dynamic Island, like the one on the iPhone. Today it shows what Spotify is playing. It’s a personal project: small, local and easy to hack on.

Is it free?

Yes. No price, no account, no trial. The source is on GitHub.

Why does macOS warn me when I open it?

shima is ad-hoc signed and not notarized by Apple, so Gatekeeper blocks the first double-click. Right-click it and choose Open once, or use the one-line Terminal command in the first-launch steps.

Does it work with Apple Music or other players?

Not yet. Version 0.1.1 talks to the Spotify desktop app only. Without Spotify running, shima simply stays invisible.

Will it slow my Mac down?

It’s built not to. While idle, it runs no timers and does no polling. While music plays it’s event-driven, and the equalizer runs at a low frame rate. You can turn the equalizer off in Settings.

What about external monitors or a Mac without a notch?

shima only uses the built-in display for now. On a built-in screen without a notch it becomes an invisible hover zone at the top centre. External monitors and clamshell mode are still on the list.

Where is it once it’s running?

There’s no Dock icon. Launch Shima again from Spotlight or Finder to open Settings, or click the gear on the open island. Quit from Settings, or from the small capsule icon in the menu bar if your menu bar has room for it.

What does “shima” mean?

島, shima, is Japanese for “island”. A small island in the notch.

Opening shima the first time.

Three steps, once. After that it opens like any other app.

Install

  1. Move it to Applications. Download the release, open the DMG and drag Shima into Applications (or unzip the ZIP and move Shima.app there). Launch at login and the Spotify permission want it to live in Applications.
  2. Right-click, then Open. In Applications, right-click Shima, choose Open, then Open again. If macOS only offers Done, go to System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway.
  3. Play something. Start Spotify and play a song. macOS asks once whether Shima may control Spotify: click Allow, and the wings appear.
Or skip step 2 in Terminal, after step 1
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Shima.app

Good to know

  • No Dock icon. shima is an accessory app. Launch it again from Spotlight to open Settings.
  • Said no to Spotify? Settings shows the status and has a button to the Automation pane in System Settings.
  • Updating. Each build is ad-hoc signed, so after installing a new version macOS may ask for the Spotify permission again.
  • Quitting. Use Quit in Settings, or run pkill -x Shima.
